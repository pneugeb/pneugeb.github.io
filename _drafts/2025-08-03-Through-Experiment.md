The central part of the problem is this: Where do you draw the line when measuring 

Let's look at the wigner's friend paradox slightly modified. Bob is outside a room, Alice is inside along with a particle with spin up or down. Both agree that the particle is in superposition, from Bob's point of view Alice and the particle also entangled. At least he can assume so, there is no size limits to where QM can be applied. If Alice now measures the particle, then for her, the spin is decided, Bob however can still assume that Alice and the Particle exist in superposition. If we now assume (as sounds reasonable) that the result Alice measured must also hold true for Bob we have a problem because Bob now can't describe the system as a superposition anymore, he now has a classical setting. This sounds at first like a semantics problem, if Alice measured it and it's in one state, Bob doesn't know but will find out when he measures. Or you could argue the system changes when Alice measures, so the superposition Bob is looking at is a new one and again the same Alice would see. This is however not the case, Bob is completely isolated and there is no mechanism in QM to transfer the information that a measurement has taken place, the measurement is part of the superposition itself. SO Bob is allowed to describe Alice as part of the superposition, which is not just statistics but as eg. the double slit experiment ha shown really a particle existing in two states at once, what does it mean for alice to exists in two states at once when she herself knows that she only exists in one state.


This was also shown in a more complex setup in a 2018 paper ["Quantum theory cannot consistently describe the
use of itself"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-018-05739-8).


Let's take the fundamental posture that the laws of physics must but the same for everyone in every reference system. That would also include our setup above. There are now two possibilities i) Alice has a definite outcomes/is not in superpos. => Bob too has a definite outcome ii) Bob sees Alice still in superpos. after she measures => Alice is still in superpos. after she measures. A solution to the first one is the logical, there is some pilot wave or force that collapses the wavefunc. universally for everybody (such a force is currently not included in QM). The second one is more difficult because what would it mean for alice to exist in superpos.? Sounds weird. But remember that electrons or other particles too exist in superpos. themselves, they don't feel anything but they're real objects. Also from Alice point of view Bob is in a superposition, maybe he already measured or he didn't.

So to summarize according to QM you're allowed to describe everything as wavefunctions and in superposition, but others are also allowed to model you as a superposition.

Let's assume a simple spin flip measurement. Particle P can be in states $\ket{0}_P$, $\ket{1}_P$: $\ket{P} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}_P + \ket{1}_P)$. Normally a measurement would yield either 0 or 1: $M\ket{P} = \ket{0}_P \vee \ket{1}_P$, where $M$ is the measurement Operator. However we are free to assume that the measurement causes an entanglement between measurement device and Particle: $\ket{MP} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\ket{0}_M\ket{0}_P + \ket{1}_M\ket{1}_P)$. The system now exists in a new Basis. Let's assume that we're also free to assume that P is fixed in $\ket{0}$. What would that mean if we want $\ket{MP}$ to be the same as before, in what basis would M live, does that equal the basis transform for P?



We could also add a third observer that measures Alice and Bob in all states.
